# Phase 2: floating soil profiles and evolving properties

The native implementation is independently qualified at the bounded scope described here. The `publication.published: false` in the immutable receipt [phase2_qualification.json](phase2_qualification.json) means *as of qualification*; the later source publication is recorded separately in [phase2_publication.json](phase2_publication.json) (source commit `71bb8fe0bb0ed2d17ee302e4502ba0511032d416` on `feature/subsurface-evolving-soil`; main integration still pending). The existing Phase 1 and 1.5 source locks remain historical and unchanged. This is a water-component API qualification, not acceptance of a complete MAPLE–SYRUP–LEAF scheduled simulation or field validation.

## Model behavior

User-defined moisture layers follow the evolving MAPLE surface. Texture is calculated from actual overlapping active-layer and voxel mass, counting material once. Per-layer water inventory stays fixed when the profile translates, except for the agreed conservative reconciliation if new field capacity or pore capacity requires water to move. Layer averaging and implicit water relocation are declared approximations, suitable for the intended relatively small event-scale bed changes. Roots retain their depth fractions; there is no plasticity or compensatory redistribution.

The actual full voxel-grid bottom is flat and impermeable. Only soil portions of nominal layers store water. Zero-soil slots contain no water and supply no roots. A configured open lower boundary exports drainage only when the profile ends strictly above rock; at rock it is closed. Gravity is always enabled in the new native production entry points, and saturated initial states are refused. Actual storm-generated saturation is allowed. Every bottom export is counted once.

The selected Saxton–Rawls (2006) method uses explicit primary sand/silt/clay, organic matter and density, with measured overrides and refusal outside its supported domain. Field capacity is evaluated at the Tietjen reference suction of −6 kPa; Green–Ampt suction and residual moisture remain explicit. Historical prescribed-property and benchmark modes are preserved. Properties are held through an event and refreshed for gross-touched columns at handoff. Actual porous depth remains current. Hydraulic rerouting has its separate existing net-elevation trigger; a refresh or checkpoint alone never reroutes.

Persistent Green–Ampt history and explicit water accounting span events. Dry days use one daily soil update. The approved wet-day exception uses short hydrology and evaporation fragments totaling exactly 86400 seconds, followed by daily plant withdrawals and audited closure. Evaporation, transpiration and growth remain inactive during wind, rain and recession. Nominal calendar days and the additional physical durations of transport events remain separate.

## Interfaces and evidence

The native NumPy reference, compiled Numba CPU and CUDA paths share the same water authority and actual MAPLE storage. See the source-specific component receipts alongside this page for numerical, conservation, mask, memory, availability, transaction and restart controls. The assembled source is identified by [the source manifest](phase2_source_manifest.json). The dependency is actual MAPLE 9f466d2cfcd52991b24496cf67f4562d6268afa9, package fbc504bbcd1ec0c02e6a96089134a06187532455ef7ba5e6f146801c8313c02e (412 files).

The [event/daily bridge](phase2_event_daily_bridge_cuda.md) carries current layer water, infiltration history, surface depth and momentum between actually completed events and daily updates. Rainfall fallback starts from an unchanged parent with the full rain amount. A matching integrated rain total alone is insufficient proof of the accepted forcing history. The [closed-parent physical checkpoint](phase2_lifecycle_checkpoint.md) preserves accepted boundaries or CLOSED days, exact day identity, cursor, withdrawal sequence, ledgers and output-frame identity. It installs a fresh head last, validates source/kernel/native-storage identities and refuses unfinished roles. The temporary sediment carrier is released rather than retained in the returned water state.

Optional every-N-day moisture diagnostics are separate from restart: absence means off, N is a positive integer, and saves follow days N, 2N, … after actual withdrawals and audited closure. Extra storm/wind time creates no nominal output days. Bounded chunked frames store current layer water and porous geometry with units and identities; unsaved times cannot be reconstructed from these frames. Seven-day tests with real intervening events and property updates save days 3 and 6 for N=3 and preserve that cadence through restart. The runnable [floating daily example](phase2_floating_daily_example.py) is a small qualification example, not a calibrated scenario or new CLI flag.

## Limits

These are native Python APIs, not additional keys accepted by historical coordinator/CLI schemas. Temporal scheduling and wind adapters belong to the coordinator; vegetation belongs to LEAF. A bounded native wind/finalizer-to-soil-refresh engineering check preserves water and history through actual bed changes, but does not qualify the integrated wind clock or a production alternating-model adapter. Wind physics is never copied into SYRUP.

General repeated-storm drying/recovery of the persistent wetting front remains bounded/unqualified; no automatic reset is inferred from rain-off, wind or a save. Pending storms, pending recession, mid-day trials and running occurrences are not handled by the closed-parent checkpoint. Moisture writer metadata remains separate. Component memory models, selected allocator/transfer measurements and actual tested geometries do not establish whole-catchment or whole-process memory fit. No field validation is claimed. Phase 2.5 vegetation projection and Phase 3 JORN/MOAB validation planning follow separately.
